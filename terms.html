Rider safety

Horse riding is a wonderful activity, but horses are large animals that can move suddenly, and riding always carries some risk of falls and injury, even with calm horses and careful teaching. We work hard to keep that risk low:

  • Riders start from age 4. Every rider is matched to a horse that suits their size, age and experience.
  • A properly fitted riding helmet must be worn whenever a rider is mounted. Ask Heidi if you need to borrow one.
  • Wear long pants and closed shoes or boots with a small heel. Sandals, slip-ons and loose jewellery are not allowed.
  • Riders follow the instructor's directions at all times, in the arena, on outrides and around the horses.
  • Children under 18 must be dropped off and collected by a parent, guardian or adult they trust, and stay in the agreed areas.
  • Please tell us about any medical condition, injury, allergy, pregnancy or medication that could affect riding before the session.
  • We may stop a session, or ask a rider not to ride, if we believe it is unsafe.

Before a first ride, riders (or a parent or guardian for riders under 18) may be asked to complete a short rider information and indemnity form.

Therapeutic riding

Therapeutic riding sessions support balance, coordination, core strength, motor skills and confidence. They add to, and do not replace, medical care, physiotherapy or occupational therapy. Please share any advice from the rider's doctor or therapist so we can plan sessions safely, and tell us about any changes in the rider's health.

At the yard

  • Do not feed the horses, walk behind them or enter paddocks and stables without the instructor.
  • Keep gates closed and voices calm around the horses.
  • Please leave dogs at home unless Heidi has agreed otherwise.
  • Visitors are on the farm at their own risk and must follow the instructor's directions.

Horse treats

  • Treats come in 250 g (R70), 500 g (R120) and 1 kg (R170) bags.
  • An order is confirmed when Heidi replies on WhatsApp. Collection, delivery and payment are arranged with her.
  • Treats are for horses only, and are a reward, not a replacement for a horse's normal feed. Feed a few at a time, and check with your vet if your horse has a special diet or health condition.
  • If something is wrong with your order, contact Heidi and we will put it right in line with the Consumer Protection Act.
